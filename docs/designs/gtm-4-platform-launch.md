## Problem Statement

Butterbase is a funded, public AI-native open-source BaaS. The product is live
and has the infrastructure. The gap is developer awareness and distribution.
Specifically: how to reach developers on Hacker News, Daily.dev, Dev.to, and
Indie Hackers — the four platforms where the target audience lives — and drive
meaningful inbound.

## Demand Evidence

- Funded (investor validation of the space)
- Public repo with GitHub stars (community signal)
- Product live and deployable today
- ICP gap identified: messaging hasn't been sharpened yet — "one stop shop" is
  a category claim every BaaS makes, not a hook. Resolve before content ships.

## Status Quo

Developers building AI-native apps are on Supabase or Firebase today.
- Supabase: Postgres-first, great DX, but AI story is bolt-on (pgvector + external LLM calls)
- Firebase: Google-ecosystem lock-in, no MCP, no agent primitives
- The gap Butterbase fills: MCP server, AI gateway, durable objects for agents, and RAG
  — all native, not duct-taped in

Supabase's newest positioning move (Oct 2025 onwards): "MCP server for your database."
They are just now starting to frame themselves as agentic infrastructure.
Butterbase has had MCP as a core primitive from day one.

## Target User & Narrowest Wedge

**Who:** Solo developer or small-team CTO building an AI-native SaaS or agent product
who is currently on Supabase and hitting the ceiling: no unified AI gateway,
no built-in MCP server, no durable objects for agent state.

**ICP gap (flagged):** User said "I'm not sure" when asked to name the actual human.
"One stop shop" is a category claim, not a hook. This MUST be resolved before
the content goes out — the wrong hook on these platforms gets ignored or downvoted.

**Narrowest wedge for GTM messaging:**
"The only BaaS with a built-in MCP server, AI gateway, and durable objects for agents."
Not: "one stop shop." Not: "like Supabase but better."

## Constraints

- Must ship content now — no 4-6 week build-in-public runway
- Two valid framing modes: founder voice ("I built this because...") or
  discovery voice ("I switched from Supabase to Butterbase and here's what I found")
- Competitive context: Supabase has $5B brand weight — direct attacks will backfire;
  technical differentiation wins

## Premises

1. Target is developers building AI-native apps on Supabase/Firebase who are hitting
   the ceiling on AI infrastructure (no MCP, no gateway, no agent primitives). AGREED.
2. "One stop shop" is not the GTM hook — "MCP-native BaaS" is. AGREED (user's own
   uncertainty about ICP confirms this needs work).
3. Each of the 4 platforms requires a different content format and different hook. AGREED.
4. One high-quality anchor piece (the Supabase wedge) generates the momentum for all 4
   platforms simultaneously. AGREED.

## Approaches Considered

### Approach A: Single Spike — Show HN now
One Show HN post this week + Dev.to tutorial + IH thread. Fastest to ship.
Rejected as standalone because a cold IH launch without journey posts gets ignored,
and HN needs a technical hook more compelling than a product announcement.

### Approach B: Build-in-Public First (6 weeks)
IH journey posts + Dev.to tutorials → warm audience → HN launch.
Rejected: user needs to move now, not in 6 weeks.

### Approach C (chosen): Supabase Wedge First, Then Show HN
Write one technically rigorous "why I switched from Supabase to Butterbase for
my AI agent backend" piece. Time the Show HN launch one week later with that
piece as proof of developer engagement. Both land in the same news cycle.

## Recommended Approach: C then A (one week apart)

**Staging gate:** Before writing anything, answer one question: can you name one
specific person who switched from Supabase to Butterbase for an AI app, or one
specific pain they hit that Butterbase solved? If yes, that person is your ICP
and your story. If no, write the tutorial anyway — the act of writing it will
surface the answer. Do not use the ICP gap as a reason to delay.

**Why C then A:** The Supabase wedge piece is the hook that explains why
Butterbase exists. It targets developers who are already searching "Supabase
alternative" with high purchase intent. On Dev.to, comparison content gets
bookmarked, and Daily.dev surfaces bookmarked content from indexed sources.
On HN, the Show HN submission references the Dev.to piece in the second comment
("we wrote up the migration story here"), which gives readers a place to go and
the comment thread a thread to pull on — the social proof is conversational,
not a vanity metric.

### Platform-by-Platform Execution

---

**HACKER NEWS — Show HN**

Format: "I built this because X wasn't enough" — first-person, founder or user voice.

Title formula (pick one):
- "Show HN: Butterbase — Open-source BaaS with a built-in MCP server for AI agents"
- "Show HN: Butterbase — I got tired of bolting AI onto Supabase, so I built a BaaS that's AI-native from the start"

Rules:
- Product must be live and tryable from the post — direct link to demo or self-host guide
- Second comment = the backstory and what makes it different from Supabase/Neon/Firebase
- Do NOT use marketing language. HN punishes passive phrases ("a platform that offers").
  Use first-person, direct: "I built X because Y was too painful."
- Timing: Tuesday–Thursday, 8–10am US Eastern. Do not post Friday or weekend.
- One submission. If it doesn't hit the front page within the first 2 hours (top 30),
  it won't — that's the HN window. Do not re-post. Fix the title and hook, wait 30 days,
  try again. The Dev.to piece still does its job regardless of HN outcome.
- Monitor and respond to comments in real time for the first 2-4 hours. This is
  the primary lever for staying power once the post is live. Block that time on your
  calendar before you submit.

The MCP angle is the hook right now. Supabase just announced MCP support;
Butterbase has had it since v1. That's a Show HN story. Lead with it.

---

**DAILY.DEV**

You cannot post directly to Daily.dev. It pulls from indexed sources (Dev.to,
personal blogs, company blogs) via an algorithm that weights upvotes, saves,
and reading time. Your action items:

1. **Publish on Dev.to first.** The Dev.to article (Supabase wedge or MCP tutorial)
   is the source Daily.dev indexes. No Dev.to post = no Daily.dev pickup.
2. **Promote the Dev.to post in AI/developer Discord servers immediately after
   publishing.** Early engagement (upvotes, comments) within 24 hours is the
   primary signal Daily.dev uses to surface new content.
3. **Relevant Discord servers to hit:** Supabase Discord (if allowed), any
   AI builders / indie hackers Discord you're already in, the Butterbase Discord.
   Do not cold-spam — participate in channels where the content is on-topic.
4. **Wait 48 hours after the Dev.to post goes live.** If the article has not
   appeared on Daily.dev within 48 hours despite strong Dev.to engagement, submit
   it manually at app.daily.dev/submit (URL submission is available to registered
   users and feeds the indexing queue).
5. **Paid fallback:** If organic placement doesn't happen within one week,
   set up a Squad at business.daily.dev. Budget: $500–$2000/month for Squad
   sponsorship; DM the Daily.dev team directly for current rates. Use this for
   sustained presence, not as a one-time launch play.

---

**DEV.TO**

The anchor piece for the entire strategy. Write it here first.

Format: Tutorial or migration story. Title: "How to give AI agents access to your
Postgres database with Butterbase MCP" OR "I migrated from Supabase to Butterbase
for my AI agent app — here's what changed."

Rules:
- If Butterbase has its own blog, publish there first, then cross-post to Dev.to
  with the canonical URL set. In Dev.to's editor: Settings → Advanced → "Canonical URL"
  → paste your blog post URL. This gives Dev.to the audience without the SEO penalty.
  If no blog exists, publish directly to Dev.to.
- Solve a specific problem the reader has. Mention Butterbase as the solution
  in-context, not in an intro paragraph or CTA footer.
- End with a working code snippet they can run today. No working code = no bookmarks.

Specific tutorial ideas ranked by search demand:
1. "How to build a RAG app with Butterbase in 10 minutes" (high demand, low supply)
2. "Butterbase MCP: Let AI agents read and write your database without glue code"
3. "Ditch Supabase's AI bolt-ons: use Butterbase's native AI gateway instead"

---

**INDIE HACKERS**

Do NOT do a cold launch post. It will be ignored.

Instead, post a journey post NOW — before the HN launch — so you arrive with at
least one prior post and a commenter trail.

Journey post formula: "We're building an AI-native BaaS in public — month X update"
OR "What we learned trying to get developers to switch from Supabase"

IH rewards:
- Revenue transparency (even small numbers — "$X MRR" outperforms "growing fast";
  even "X apps deployed on Butterbase" or "X Discord members" is better than nothing)
- Specific failures and what you changed
- Consistency over spikes — one post per week for 2-3 weeks before the HN launch
  gives you a commenter trail; readers can see you're active before they engage

**Pre-launch IH schedule (start immediately, before the launch week):**
- Week -2: "Why we're building an AI-native BaaS in public" (origin story)
- Week -1: "What we learned from talking to Supabase developers about agent infrastructure"
- Week 0 (launch week): "We just launched Butterbase on HN — here's what happened"

The IH post and the HN launch should reference each other. IH readers click through
to HN; HN readers land on IH and see a founder with a track record. Pre-drafting
both the week-0 IH post and the HN second comment before launch day prevents
split attention when you're managing live HN comments.

### Pre-Launch (Week -2 and -1, start immediately)

**Week -2:** IH post #1 — origin story. Title: "Why I built an AI-native BaaS instead
  of using Supabase for my agent app" or "Building Butterbase in public — why we started."
**Week -1:** IH post #2 — learning post. Title: "What developers said when I told
  them I was building a Supabase alternative with a built-in MCP server."
**Week-0 IH post (pre-draft before Day 5):** Title: "We launched Butterbase on Hacker
  News today — here's what happened in the first 4 hours." Write the structure before
  launch; fill in the actual numbers after the HN window closes (8am-noon Day 5).
**Any day before Day 5:** Pre-draft the Show HN second comment. Have it ready to paste
  within 2 minutes of submission.

**Timing note:** If you are starting today (Week -2 begins now), you have 14 days
  before the HN launch on Day 5 of launch week. That is tight but workable: 2 IH
  posts, 1 Dev.to tutorial, and pre-drafted launch materials. If you cannot start
  the IH posts today, shift the HN launch one week later — the warm-up matters more
  than the launch date.

### Launch Week Sequence

**Day 1 (Monday):** Publish Dev.to tutorial ("Butterbase MCP: Let AI agents use
  your database without glue code"). Set canonical URL if cross-posting from blog.
**Day 1 (afternoon):** Share in Discord communities where the tutorial is on-topic:
  Discord servers you're already active in (AI builders, indie hackers, your own
  Butterbase Discord). If you are already a member of the Supabase Discord, check
  whether they allow third-party tool sharing in #showcase or #build-in-public
  before posting — do not cold-join and immediately post. Do not cold-post to
  unfamiliar servers.
**Day 2:** Check Dev.to engagement. Respond to every comment. Share on LinkedIn if
  the team has a following there.
**Day 3:** Check if article surfaced on Daily.dev (search your title). If not, submit
  at app.daily.dev/submit manually.
**Day 4 (Monday night):** Final check — tutorial link works, demo link works, self-host
  guide is accurate. HN will send real traffic; broken links are a top-30 killer.
**Day 5 (Tuesday, 8am ET):** Submit Show HN. Post the pre-drafted second comment
  within 2 minutes of submission. Block your calendar 8am-noon for comment monitoring.
**Day 6 (Wednesday):** Publish the pre-drafted IH week-0 launch post ("We just launched
  on HN — here's what happened"). Link to the HN thread.
**Day 7:** Dev.to follow-up: "Show HN recap — what developers asked about Butterbase."
  This doubles as the content that keeps the Daily.dev algorithm warm.
**Ongoing:** Daily.dev pickup is driven by Dev.to engagement, not time. Keep posting.

## Open Questions

1. **ICP gap:** Who specifically is the target developer? The messaging above assumes
   "Supabase migrator building an AI-native app" — this needs to be validated with
   at least one real customer quote before the content goes out.
2. **Framing mode:** Founder voice vs. discovery voice? If there's a real user
   who migrated from Supabase and would write a guest post, the discovery frame
   is more credible on HN.
3. **MRR/traction numbers for IH:** How much can you share? Even "X apps built on
   Butterbase" or "X developers in the Discord" moves the needle on IH authenticity.

## Success Criteria

Baselines needed before setting targets (pull these before launch week):
- Current GitHub star count (200+ new stars in launch week is a reasonable target
  for a Show HN front-page hit from a repo with 500–2000 existing stars; adjust down
  if the repo is newer)
- Current Discord member count (use as social proof in IH posts)

Targets:
- Show HN: front page (top 30), 50+ points within 2 hours
- Dev.to: 200+ reactions in the first week (enough for Daily.dev indexing signal)
- Daily.dev: article appears within 48 hours of Dev.to publication
- IH: week-0 post gets 10+ comments; pre-launch posts each get 3+ comments
- Overall: 200+ new GitHub stars in the 7-day launch window

## The Assignment

Two things, in order:

1. **Today:** Post IH journey post #1 (origin story). 200–400 words, honest, first-person.
   This starts the clock on the pre-launch warm-up. Do not skip this step — it's what

2. **By Friday:** Publish the Dev.to tutorial. One problem. One working code snippet.

   that as a reason to delay past Friday.

The HN post comes after both of these exist. Not before.
